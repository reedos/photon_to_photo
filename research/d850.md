# Nikon D850 — sensor and body geometry sources

Research pass by agent "d850", 09/28/2026. Companion to `data/d850.json`, which carries the same
figures machine-readably as `{ v, unit, ev, src, loc, accessed }`. This is the sensor the site's
DSLR body uses (its look stays generic/unbranded); the mirrorless body uses the Nikon Z8's sensor
and Z-mount geometry, in `data/z8.json`/`research/z8.md`.

All web lookups were done 09/28/2026; that is the `accessed` date everywhere below and in the JSON.
Evidence kinds follow the project convention already used in `research/sensor-sources.md`: spec,
vendor, reported, derived, assumed.

## Method for the read-noise/PDR data

The brief pointed at the same `.local/read-noise/*.cjs` scripts an earlier agent used for
`data/read-noise.json`. That agent's own `research/read-noise.md` records that a plain `curl`
fetch of `RN_e.htm`/`PDR.htm`/`Sensor_Characteristics.htm` returns byte-identical series data to a
Playwright-rendered page — the numbers are literal JS array data written into the page's own HTML,
not something requiring a JS engine to read. This pass reused that finding directly: it re-fetched
the same three URLs with plain `curl` (`.local/d850/captures/{rn_e,pdr,sensorchar}.html`), which
also already contain every other camera the site tracks (445/552/6 series respectively), then
extracted the Nikon D850 and Nikon Z 8 entries from those same three files with a bracket-balanced
scan + `new Function('return '+text)()`, adapted from `.local/read-noise/parse_rn.cjs` and
`build_tables.cjs` — see `.local/d850/parse_rn.cjs` (copied unmodified) and
`.local/d850/build_d850.cjs` / `.local/z8/build_z8.cjs` (adapted per-camera table builders).

Two D850 series exist on `RN_e.htm`: `Nikon D850_14` (mechanical shutter) and `Nikon D850(ES)_14`
(electronic shutter, i.e. "Silent live view photography" mode). Both were extracted; the
mechanical-shutter series is used as the primary record since that's the D850's normal operating
mode, with the ES series kept as a same-camera cross-check (`sensor.electronicShutterES` in the
JSON) — the two are nearly identical.

**DCG-switch ISO** is derived the same way as `data/read-noise.json`: the steepest negative slope
of `log2(read noise) vs log2(ISO)` between adjacent, unmarked, in-normal-analog-range points. For
the D850 that lands on **ISO 318 → 400** (essentially 320 → 400), a -3.76 stops/stop cliff — matched
almost exactly by the electronic-shutter series (also 318 → 400, -3.82 stops/stop), and
coincidentally at the identical ISO step the Sony a6300/a6400/a6500 family lands on in
`data/read-noise.json`.

## Sensor

**Chip identity.** Nikon has never named the D850's sensor supplier or part number. It's widely
reported at launch and since (DPReview headline: "Nikon D850 offers 45.7MP BSI FX-format sensor")
as a Sony-fabricated BSI full-frame chip, generally discussed in the same 45.7MP-class BSI sensor
family as the Sony a7R III's (both announced in 2017), but this pass found no vendor datasheet or
confirmed teardown naming an exact Sony IMX part for the D850, unlike the a7R IV/IMX455 case
`research/sensor-sources.md` documents. Not claimed as a specific chip here.
<https://www.dpreview.com/news/4840194993/nikon-d850-offers-45-7mp-bsi-fx-format-sensor-7-fps-bursts-4k-video>

**Active area / pixel count / pixel pitch** (reported), Wikipedia infobox
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_D850>, cross-checked against DPReview's own spec page
<https://www.dpreview.com/products/nikon/slrs/nikon_d850/specifications>:
- Sensor 35.9 × 23.9 mm, 45.4 effective MP (8256 × 5504). Nikon's own marketing commonly rounds
  this to "45.7 million total pixels" (total, not effective, pixel count) — both figures appear
  across sources; not resolved further, both worth knowing if the engine team sees either number
  elsewhere.
- Pixel pitch reported directly at 4.35 µm by <https://apotelyt.com/camera-specs/nikon-d850-sensor-pixels>;
  cross-checked by computing `sqrt(35.9mm * 23.9mm / 45.4e6 px) ≈ 4.347 µm`, matching to rounding.
- BSI (back-illuminated) — Nikon's first DSLR with a back-illuminated full-frame sensor, per the
  DPReview headline above.

**Base ISO / range** (reported): native ISO 64–25,600, expandable 32–102,400.
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_D850> (infobox) and
<https://www.dpreview.com/products/nikon/slrs/nikon_d850/specifications> ("Native ISO: 64-25,600",
"Boosted ISO: 32-102,400").

**ADC bit depth** (reported): raw files are 14-bit lossless-compressed, with a 12-bit option.
Wikipedia and DPReview's spec page agree: "Raw (Nikon NEF, 12 or 14 bit)".

**Full well capacity** (reported, two non-averaged figures, both from photonstophotos.net):
- RN_e.htm series `Nikon D850_14`, Highcharts `fwc` field: **59,126 e-**.
- Sensor_Characteristics.htm ("DxOMark Derived Sensor Characteristics"), a separate dataset Claff
  derives from DxOMark's own published scores: `{ name: 'Nikon D850_14', unitygain: 233.0,
  fwc: 60912, qe: 44, readnoise: 0.9, adcnoise: 1.4, isoinvariantat: 683 }` — **60,912 e-**.
- The two are close but not identical, the same pattern `research/read-noise.md` documents for the
  a7R IV/a6400 (two independent derivations of the same camera by the same site). Recorded
  separately in the JSON, per this project's "record both, don't average" rule.

**Conversion gain / unity-gain ISO** (reported): **233** (RN_e.htm `unityEv` converted), matching
the Sensor_Characteristics.htm DxOMark-derived `unitygain: 233.0` almost exactly — the same
strong-internal-consistency pattern `research/read-noise.md` found for the a7R IV (217 vs 217.1).

**Read noise in electrons vs ISO, and the DCG switch** (reported/derived): full per-ISO table in
`data/d850.json`, `readNoise.points`. Read noise falls from ~4.1 e- at base ISO 64 to a floor around
~1.0–1.2 e- above the DCG switch. **DCG switch: ISO 318 → 400** (see Method above).

**Photographic Dynamic Range (PDR)** (reported): full curve in the same `points` array (`pdrEv`
field), from PDR.htm series `Nikon D850`. PDR at base ISO 64: **11.63 EV**; the chart's own
`lowLightEv` marker for this camera is 10.3629.

**DxOMark scores** (reported), <https://www.dxomark.com/nikon-d850-sensor-review-first-dslr-hit-100-points/>:
overall score **100** (first DSLR sensor to reach 100), Color Depth (Portrait) **26.4 bits**,
Dynamic Range (Landscape) **14.8 EV**, Low-Light ISO (Sports) **2660**, all at base ISO.

**QE** (reported, DxOMark-derived, not a Nikon-published figure): Sensor_Characteristics.htm gives
`qe: 44` (%) for `Nikon D850_14`. Per-channel (R/G/B) QE curves were not found published anywhere in
text-extractable form for this camera, same gap `research/sensor-sources.md` flags for the other
three cameras.

**What Claff's `qe` field means** (added 10/11/2026, score loop r2). Bill Claff's "DxOMark Gain Analysis"
(<https://www.photonstophotos.net/Investigations/DxOMark_Gain_Analysis.htm>, prepared 11/05/2014) derives the
figure from full-well capacity and DxOMark's measured ISO, and says the result is "something more like the
product of QE and fill factor" (accessed 10/11/2026). So it is an effective, whole-pixel figure. The engine now
applies it without the separate 0.97 microlens fill factor, which had counted that loss twice (about 3% fewer
electrons). Claff does not state the wavelength or light source behind the number, and the same page warns that
an overestimated gain inflates derived FWC and QE. Using it as the green channel's spectral peak is still this
project's interpretation, and the model's absolute sensitivity has not been checked against a sourced measured
or saturation ISO for this body (no text-extractable DxOMark value was found; the photonstophotos Measured ISO
chart does not expose its numbers as text).

**ISO-invariance point** (reported, DxOMark-derived): Sensor_Characteristics.htm gives
`isoinvariantat: 683` — Claff's own estimate of the ISO above which raising analog gain further
buys essentially nothing over a digital push in post.

**Readout / rolling-shutter time** (reported, primary measurement): 64 ms for the full frame in electronic
silent shutter Mode 1. Jim Kasson's post on D850 electronic-shutter speed, the last word blog,
10/30/2017 (<https://blog.kasson.com/d850/how-fast-is-the-nikon-850-electronic-shutter/>, read directly
10/01/2026): an analog oscilloscope trace at 10 ms per division spans 6.4 divisions across the full 8256 × 5504
Mode 1 frame. Mode 2 is a crop and is excluded. This matches `data/d850.json`
`sensor.readout.electronicFullFrameMs` (ev `reported`, confidence "primary measurement directly read and
verified"). An earlier pass of this note called the figure low confidence because the post returned HTTP 403
then and the number came only from a search summary of a DPReview forum thread
(<https://www.dpreview.com/forums/thread/4494255>); that was superseded when the post was read (updated
10/11/2026, score loop r2). The horshack-dpreview RollingShutter table
(<https://horshack-dpreview.github.io/RollingShutter/>) still has no D850 row. The figure applies to that mode on
the tested body, not to every readout mode.

**Fill factor** (assumed): same reasoning as every BSI sensor in `data/sensors.json` — no
D850-specific number published, ~0.97 assumed on the "approaches 100% for BSI" basis, cited to
<https://www.tucsen.com/learning/demystifying-specifications-sensor-model/>.

### Raw black level (added 10/11/2026, score-loop r1)

- **400 DN at base ISO (14-bit raw), reported.** Jim Kasson, "Nikon D850 histograms vs ISO setting", the last
  word blog, 10/30/2017 (https://blog.kasson.com/d850/nikon-d850-histograms-vs-iso-setting/, accessed 10/11/2026).
  Full-frame dark-field histograms at 1/1600 s. He writes that at base ISO the black point is at 400 counts, and
  that on the transition from ISO 1250 to ISO 1600 it moves lower; he does not give the lower value.
- Recorded as `sensor.blackLevelDn` in `data/d850.json` with `validIsoBelow: 1600`. The engine labels the figure
  `reported` below ISO 1600 and `assumed` (same 400 DN kept) from ISO 1600 up.
- Before this, the D850 used the project's assumed 512 DN. With 512 DN the 14-bit ADC clipped at ISO 64 below the
  cited full well (16383 - 512 = 15871 DN of headroom); with 400 DN the headroom is 15983 DN.
- A third-party measurement, not a Nikon specification. No Nikon-published black level was found.

## Body geometry — Nikon F-mount

**Flange focal distance / throat diameter** (reported), directly quoted from Wikipedia's technical
summary: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_F-mount> — "a three-lug bayonet mount with a **44 mm
throat** and a flange to focal plane distance of **46.5 mm**." No separate "image plane position"
figure distinct from flange focal distance was found anywhere — the sensor plane simply sits at the
flange-to-focal-plane distance behind the mount face; recorded as `imagePlaneNote` in the JSON
rather than as a distinct number, since none exists to cite.

**Shutter** (reported): mechanical shutter range **1/8000 s to 30 s, bulb**
(<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_D850>); flash sync speed **1/250 s**
(<https://www.dpreview.com/products/nikon/slrs/nikon_d850/specifications>). Nikon does not publish
a raw shutter-curtain travel-time figure for the D850 in any source checked in this pass; flash sync
speed is recorded as the closest published proxy (the classic "how fast do the curtains cross the
frame" number photographers actually use), not a direct curtain-travel-time measurement — flagged
in `meta.openProblems`.

**Mirror / viewfinder** (reported): standard DSLR mechanical reflex mirror with an optical
pentaprism viewfinder, **100% coverage**, **0.75× magnification** — Nikon's own marketing calls this
the highest viewfinder magnification of any Nikon DSLR.
<https://www.dpreview.com/products/nikon/slrs/nikon_d850/specifications>. The D850 also supports
mirror-up shooting and a fully electronic "Silent live view photography" mode (mirror raised,
electronic shutter) — this is the same operating mode as the `Nikon D850(ES)_14` photonstophotos
series recorded under `sensor.electronicShutterES`.
<https://www.the-digital-picture.com/Reviews/Nikon-D850.aspx>

**Body dimensions / weight / processor / release** (reported), Wikipedia infobox
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_D850>: 146 × 124 × 78.5 mm; 915 g body only / 1,005 g with
battery; Nikon EXPEED 5 processor; released 08/24/2017; magnesium-alloy weather-sealed body
(<https://www.dpreview.com/products/nikon/slrs/nikon_d850/specifications>).

**Monitor and dimensions** (spec, added 10/11/2026, score loop r2): Nikon's own D850 online manual,
Technical Notes > Specifications (<https://onlinemanual.nikonimglib.com/d850/en/19_technical_notes_11.html>,
accessed 10/11/2026), gives the monitor as "8-cm/3.2-in., approx. 2359k-dot (XGA) tilting TFT
touch-sensitive LCD" and the camera as "Approx. 146 × 124 × 78.5 mm", with no note on what the depth
includes (the only "excluding projections" on the page is the MH-25a charger's). Derived for the model
(`blender/dslr_v2.py`, `bodykit.screen_unit`): XGA is 1024 × 768, a 4:3 panel (and 1024 × 768 × 3 =
2,359,296 dots, the 2359k given), so a 3.2 in. (81.28 mm) diagonal is 65.0 × 48.8 mm of picture. The model's
glass was 77 × 54.4 mm (3.7 in.) before this; the frame's opening keeps that size, with a black mask round
the picture.

## Open problems

1. **Readout/rolling-shutter time (64 ms)**: resolved. Kasson's oscilloscope post was read directly on
   10/01/2026 (see above); the figure covers electronic silent Mode 1 only.
2. **Chip identity** is not confirmed to a specific Sony part number, unlike the a7R IV/IMX455
   case — recorded as "reported, Sony-fabricated BSI, same generation family as a7R III" and no
   further.
3. **Curtain travel time** was not found published by Nikon; flash sync speed (1/250s) stands in as
   the closest public proxy, explicitly flagged as not the same quantity.
4. **Effective vs. total pixel count**: sources split between 45.4MP (effective, Wikipedia/DPReview)
   and the commonly-quoted 45.7MP (Nikon's own marketing "total pixels" figure) — both recorded,
   not resolved to one number.
5. Two non-averaged full-well figures (59,126 e- vs 60,912 e-) and a marker-symbol simplification
   (`'triangle'` recorded generically rather than distinguishing `'triangle-down'`/`'triangle-up'`
   as `data/read-noise.json` does) are both noted in `data/d850.json`'s own notes/meta fields.

## Depth, eyepiece, prism height and grip profile: searched, not found (score loop r4, 10/11/2026)

The round-3 reviewers asked for a published D850 eyepiece or eyecup protrusion, a scaled side or front view for the
prism and the grip, and published grip dimensions. Searched 10/11/2026 (web search, then a direct read of Nikon's
specification page); nothing sourced turned up for any of them:

- **Depth.** Nikon's online manual, "Specifications"
  (<https://onlinemanual.nikonimglib.com/d850/en/19_technical_notes_11.html>, read 10/11/2026): "Approx. 146 x 124 x
  78.5 mm (5.8 x 4.9 x 3.1 in.)" with no footnote or qualifier on that row. The words "excluding projections" appear
  on the same page only in the MH-25a charger's row, not the camera's. Nikon USA's specification tab
  (<https://www.nikonusa.com/p/d850/1585/overview>) and Wikipedia
  (<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_D850>) repeat 78.5 mm; Imaging Resource and Apotelyt round it to 79 mm. None
  says what the depth is measured to.
- **Eyepiece or eyecup protrusion.** None found. The only eyepiece figure on Nikon's page is the eyepoint, "17 mm (-1.0
  m-1; from center surface of viewfinder eyepiece lens)", which is where the eye sits, not how far the eyecup stands
  out. Retail listings for the DK-17 and DK-17F eyecups give a package size (about 30 x 30 x 70 mm, unlabeled) and a DPReview
  forum post says the DK-17M is 4 mm thicker than the stock piece: neither is a protrusion.
- **Prism height, grip depth, grip recess.** None found: no dimensioned side or front elevation of the D850, no grip-only
  depth, and no recess dimension from Nikon, DPReview, Imaging Resource or the retailers. Nikon's manual shows the front
  and side views only in perspective, without a scale.

What the model does about it, stated plainly:

- **The depth is not reconciled.** The shell's 78.5 mm runs from the rear plate (y -24) to the grip front (y 54.5). The
  eyepiece housing stands 4 mm behind the plate and the eyecup 14.6 mm behind it (model total 93.6 mm). Under the CIPA
  rule as reported (research/exteriors.md, "Body depth convention") a 14.6 mm protrusion on 78.5 mm could not be left
  out, so either the real eyepiece is shorter than the model's or Nikon's 78.5 mm does not follow that rule. The
  eyepiece length is the model's own, not sourced, and was not changed. The Models page now says what its 78.5 mm
  covers (public/models/bodies.json `depthNote`) instead of calling the whole model "built to the published envelope".
- **The prism is 124 mm tall in total** (z -45 to 79, the published height), so its rise above the shoulder (z 47) is
  whatever that leaves; no separate prism height is published, and the model's 32 mm is unverified.
- **The grip's profile is stylized.** Its plan (inner flank leaving the front face at x 42-43, front 64 mm out) comes
  from the top drawing (below); its finger channel, its forward bulge and its elevation are the model's own shapes, not
  traced, and no recess or flare was added because there is nothing to size it from.
- **The prism's cheeks** follow the rear drawing at two heights (48 mm across at z 61.6, 44 mm at z 68.3) and the
  54 mm root; between and above those points the cheek is a smooth spline (blender/bodykit.py `smooth_curve`) with two
  stylized control points (z 53.5 and 74.4) that only set how it leaves the root and rolls onto the shoe's top. The nose
  rake in elevation and the plan corners are rounded with larger radii (7 to 11 mm) as the model's own choice.

## Eyepiece length, grip and mount boss: round 5 (score loop r5, 10/11/2026)

Searched again (web search, 10/11/2026) for a D850 eyecup or eyepiece protrusion, a dimensioned side or rear elevation,
a DK-17 / DK-19 dimension and a grip-only depth: retailer and comparison-site listings repeat "146 x 124 x 78.5 mm" (some
round to 79 mm) with no statement of what the depth includes, and none gives a protrusion, a grip depth or a recess. Nikon's
top drawing (`img/d850_bd_parts_top_add.png`, read in the browser, nothing downloaded) was looked at again: its view is
tilted, so depth in it cannot be scaled from the 0.2877 mm per pixel that holds across; it does show the eyepiece housing
standing out of the rear outline by a few millimeters (about 3 to 7, depending on how the tilt is read), not 14.6.

What changed in the model, stated as what it is:

- **The eyepiece now stands 7.8 mm behind the rear plate** (the housing 4 mm of it, the squared rubber block to 6.0 mm and
  the round cup to 7.8 mm), not 14.6 mm. This is **the model's own length, not a measurement**. It is the ceiling that the
  CIPA rule as reported (research/exteriors.md, "Body depth convention") allows: a protrusion may be left out of a depth
  only if it stands out no more than 10% of it, which for 78.5 mm is 7.85 mm; it is also within what the tilted top drawing
  shows. Overall the model is now 86.8 mm deep with the grip rubber and eyecup (it was 93.5); its shell is still 78.5 mm
  from the rear plate to the grip front. `blender/dslr_v2.py` (`EYE_STAND`, `EYE_BLOCK`), pinned by
  `src/scene/bodies/blender-glb.test.ts`, "dslr.glb eyepiece length".
- **The grip's profile is still the model's own.** No source places a finger recess, a flare or a forward bulge, so none
  was added; `public/models/bodies.json` `shapeNote` and the grip's note on the Models page say the profile is generic (its
  plan follows the top drawing; its channel and bulge are the model's own). The mount boss (5.5 to 6 mm tall, 62 mm across
  for the DSLR) is likewise the model's own and is named in the same note.

## Grip, hump rake and mount boss: round 6 (score loop r6, 10/11/2026)

The round-5 reviewers asked once more for the grip's profile, the hump's front rake and the mount boss's height and diameter,
calibrated from photographs of the real body against dimensions already in the repo (146 mm width, 44 mm throat, 72 or 76 mm lens
barrel). Searched again (web search, 10/14/2026: "Nikon D850 side view dimensioned drawing grip depth mount protrusion eyepiece",
and "D850 dimensions drawing PDF L-bracket CAD STEP model grip front to lens mount flange distance"). Results: Wikipedia, Apotelyt,
DSLRBodies, Imaging Resource, a retailer's specification PDF and Full Compass all repeat "146 x 124 x 78.5 mm" (one rounds to 79
mm) and the sensor's 35.9 x 23.9 mm; none gives a side or front elevation, a grip depth, a recess, the boss or the eyepiece length,
and no CAD, STEP or L-bracket drawing of the body turned up (the one CAD hit was a different part's forum thread). Nikon's manual
still shows the front and side only in perspective.

What can be derived from photographs without a scaled elevation? Nothing that is more than a guess: a three-quarter photograph
has no single scale (the grip front stands 10 to 15 mm nearer the lens than the mount face, so a scale taken from the barrel
diameter is wrong for it by 10 to 20%), and the repo's reference photographs (public/media, the Commons D850 photograph) are
not square on. So **no grip profile, finger recess, flare, rake angle, boss height or boss diameter was added or changed**; each
stays the model's own stylized shape, named as such in `public/models/bodies.json` `shapeNote` (shown on the Models page).

What changed instead, with no new dimension (every number is a round or a clearance, not a measurement of the camera):

- **The visor's lower corners.** The prism housing's nose was 48 mm across at its top (z 53) while the hump's cheek there is 52.8
  mm (the traced spline), so a 2.4 mm ledge ran along each side where the housing's slanted flank met the hump, and the
  housing's plan had 1.5 mm corners against the hump's 6 mm. The housing's top is now as wide as the cheek there (53.2 mm,
  the traced cheek's own width), its plan front corners are 9 mm rounds and its top corners 5 mm rounds, and the join of its
  flank with the cheek has a 4.5 mm fillet. The rake and the cheek spline are unchanged.
- **The cutaway hump** now keeps to the prism (see docs/score-loop/r6-blender.md). The prism is the one derived from the
  screen's 25 mm depth; nothing about it changed.
- **Mount ring.** The lock pin and screw heads sit in 0.5 mm pockets in the face, so the ring's front is the flange plane (46.5)
  and nothing stands past it. The boss is untouched.

## First-party specifications (score loop r6, 10/14/2026)

The reviewers noted that the body's foundational specs leaned on Wikipedia, DPReview and apotelyt.com. Nikon's own pages were
