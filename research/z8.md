# Nikon Z8 — sensor and body geometry sources

Research pass by agent "d850" (task extended mid-run by the lead to also cover the Z8, the
project's mirrorless-body pick), 09/28/2026. Companion to `data/z8.json`, same shape/conventions as
`data/d850.json` and `research/d850.md` — read that file first for the shared read-noise extraction
method, since it's identical here. This file adds the Z8-specific sourcing and an explicit
D850-vs-Z8 contrast (also captured machine-readably as `data/z8.json`'s `contrastWithD850` block).

All web lookups were done 09/28/2026; that is the `accessed` date everywhere below and in the JSON.

## Method

Identical extraction pipeline to `research/d850.md`: the same three curl-fetched
photonstophotos.net pages already saved at `.local/d850/captures/{rn_e,pdr,sensorchar}.html`
(which carry every tracked camera, not just the D850) were reused rather than re-fetched, and the
Nikon Z8's series pulled out of them with the same bracket-balanced-scan parser
(`.local/z8/build_z8.cjs`, adapted from `.local/d850/build_d850.cjs`). Series names: `Nikon Z 8_14`
(RN_e.htm), `Nikon Z 8` (PDR.htm), `Nikon Z 8_14` (Sensor_Characteristics.htm). A DX-crop series,
`Nikon Z 8(DX)`, also exists on PDR.htm and was not used, for the same reason `research/read-noise.md`
skipped the a7R IV's APS-C crop series — this project's full-frame picks use the native full-frame
readout.

## Sensor

**Chip identity** (reported): Nikon does not name the Z8's sensor supplier or part number. It is
universally reported — DPReview's Z8 review calls it a "45.7MP Stacked CMOS sensor that has
parallel readout paths, one for images or video and another to provide the viewfinder feed with
minimal lag" — as sharing its sensor generation with the flagship Z9 (announced ~18 months
earlier). No vendor datasheet or confirmed teardown naming an exact part was found, the same gap
`research/d850.md` documents for the D850.
<https://www.dpreview.com/reviews/nikon-z8-review>

**Active area / pixel count / pixel pitch** (reported), Wikipedia infobox
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_Z8>: FX full-frame, 35.9 × 23.9 mm, 45.4 effective MP (8256 ×
5504) — **essentially identical numbers to the D850's own record**, expected since both are Nikon
FX bodies from a closely related sensor generation. Pixel pitch not independently published for the
Z8; derived the same way as the D850's: `sqrt(35.9mm × 23.9mm / 45.4e6 px) ≈ 4.347 µm`, i.e. the
same 4.35 µm figure the D850 reports directly.

**Architecture** (reported): stacked, back-illuminated CMOS — the key structural difference from the
D850's non-stacked BSI sensor. Stacking splits the pixel array die from a separate logic/memory die
bonded beneath it, enabling much faster on-chip readout (see Readout below) at some cost to full
well (see Full well below).

**Base ISO / range** (reported): native 64–25,600, expandable 32–102,400 —
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_Z8> infobox. One WebSearch-tool summary paraphrased a source
as "native sensitivity of ISO 64-256,000," almost certainly a digit-order slip for the boosted
32-102,400 range; not used, since it conflicts with both the Wikipedia infobox and this project's
own photonstophotos extraction (`normalAnalogRangeIso: [63, 25600]`, matching the conventional
64–25,600 figure to the chart's 1/3-stop grid).

**Full well capacity** (reported, two non-averaged figures): RN_e.htm series `Nikon Z 8_14`,
`fwc: 54323` e-; Sensor_Characteristics.htm (DxOMark-derived) row `{ name: 'Nikon Z 8_14',
unitygain: 216.9, fwc: 56277, qe: 44, readnoise: 1.0, adcnoise: 5.1, isoinvariantat: 2248 }`, i.e.
56,277 e-. Both are noticeably **lower** than the D850's two figures (59,126 / 60,912 e-) despite
essentially the same active area and pixel count — plausible given the stacked design typically
trades some full-well capacity for the extra in-pixel/near-pixel readout circuitry, but no source
checked in this pass states that causal link explicitly for this specific camera pair, so it's
flagged as a plausible-but-unconfirmed explanation, not stated as fact in the JSON.

**Conversion gain / unity-gain ISO** (reported): **222** (RN_e.htm `unityEv` converted), close to
the Sensor_Characteristics.htm DxOMark-derived `unitygain: 216.9`.

**DCG switch** (derived, same slope method as `research/d850.md`): **ISO 400 → 503** (essentially
400 → 500), read noise dropping from 5.502 e- to 1.257 e-, a **-6.45 stops/stop** cliff. This is
both a later switch point and a much sharper cliff than the D850's ISO 318 → 400 (-3.76 stops/stop),
and steeper than any of the four cameras already in `data/sensors.json`/`data/read-noise.json`
(-1.94 to -3.82 stops/stop range). No source checked explains why; recorded as an observation for
the engine team in `data/z8.json`'s notes.

**Read noise / PDR curves** (reported): full tables in `data/z8.json` `readNoise.points`. Read noise
sits around 6–7 e- below the DCG switch (notably higher than the D850's ~3.5–4.6 e- in the same ISO
band) and falls to the same ~1.0–1.2 e- floor above it. PDR at base ISO 64 is 11.31 EV (vs the
D850's 11.63 EV) — the PDR curve visibly dips at the ISO 318/400 points right before the DCG switch
(9.09, then 8.73 EV) and jumps back up at the switch itself (9.54 EV at ISO 503) — the same
"PDR recovers exactly at the DCG step" pattern visible in the D850's and a7R IV's own curves in
`research/read-noise.md`.

**DxOMark scores** (reported), <https://www.dxomark.com/nikon-z8-sensor-test/>: overall **98**
(vs the D850's 100 — first DSLR sensor to hit 100), Color Depth (Portrait) **26.3 bits**, Dynamic
Range (Landscape) **14.2 EV**, Low-Light ISO (Sports) **2548**, all at base ISO 64.

**QE, ISO-invariance** (reported, DxOMark-derived, not Nikon-published): Sensor_Characteristics.htm
gives `qe: 44` (identical to the D850's own 44%) and `isoinvariantat: 2248` — more than triple the
D850's 683, consistent with the later DCG switch point.

**What Claff's `qe` field means** (added 10/11/2026, score loop r2). Bill Claff's "DxOMark Gain Analysis"
(<https://www.photonstophotos.net/Investigations/DxOMark_Gain_Analysis.htm>, prepared 11/05/2014) derives the
figure from full-well capacity and DxOMark's measured ISO, and says the result is "something more like the
product of QE and fill factor" (accessed 10/11/2026). So it is an effective, whole-pixel figure. The engine now
applies it without the separate 0.97 microlens fill factor, which had counted that loss twice (about 3% fewer
electrons). Claff does not state the wavelength or light source behind the number, and the same page warns that
an overestimated gain inflates derived FWC and QE. Using it as the green channel's spectral peak is still this
project's interpretation, and the model's absolute sensitivity has not been checked against a sourced measured
or saturation ISO for this body (no text-extractable DxOMark value was found; the photonstophotos Measured ISO
chart does not expose its numbers as text).

**adcnoise anomaly**: the Sensor_Characteristics.htm row's `adcnoise` field is 5.1 e- for the Z8,
markedly higher than the D850's 1.4 e- or any of the four cameras already recorded in
`data/read-noise.json` (0.9–2.7 e-). Recorded as-is; not explained by any source checked in this
pass. A plausible but unconfirmed guess (EXPEED 7's different downstream ADC/processing pipeline vs
the D850's EXPEED 5) is noted in `data/z8.json`'s `meta.openProblems` explicitly labeled as
unsourced speculation, not fact.

**Readout / rolling-shutter speed** (reported, secondary source): Thom Hogan's "Sensor Read-Out Speeds"
article (<https://www.zsystemuser.com/z-mount-cameras/z-camera-articles/sensor-read-out-speeds.html>),
read directly in the score loop's round 6 (10/14/2026; the body text sits past the first 100,000
characters of the page), lists the still-photography full-sensor readout for the largest raw files
with the electronic shutter as **Z8 3.7 ms (1/268)** and Z9 3.7 ms (1/268). It is a third-party
measured list, not a Nikon figure, so the record is tagged `secondary`. This replaces the earlier
3.6 ms value, which had come from search-tool summaries when the article body was unreadable. A
DPReview forum thread (<https://www.dpreview.com/forums/thread/4752433>, HTTP 403 to our tools; cited
by search-tool summaries) reports the same ~1/268 for stills and drops to 1/90 at 60 fps and 1/173 at
120 fps; those are mode-dependent and not recorded. The horshack-dpreview RollingShutter table
(<https://horshack-dpreview.github.io/RollingShutter/>) carries no Z8 row.

**Mechanical shutter**: the Z8 has **none** — confirmed by multiple independent reviews (DPReview,
Digital Camera World's "Opinion: Nixing the Nikon Z8's mechanical shutter was a genius move," and
others). It retains a shutter-shield mechanism that closes over the sensor only when the camera is
powered off, purely to keep dust off the sensor during lens changes — not a capture-path shutter.
Electronic shutter range 1/32000s–900s (Wikipedia infobox). Flash sync speed **1/200s**
(base, up to 1/8000s with high-speed sync) — slightly slower base sync than the D850's mechanical
1/250s, despite having no curtain at all; the limiting factor is the electronic rolling-shutter
readout/flash-duration relationship, not a curtain-travel-time constraint.

**Fill factor** (assumed): same ~0.97 assumption and Tucsen citation as every other BSI sensor in
this project, including the D850 — no Z8-specific figure published.

### Raw black level (searched 10/11/2026, score-loop r1)

- **No cited figure found.** A web search for a Z8 raw black level or dark-frame black point (Nikon manual
  specifications pages, DPReview threads, zsystemuser.com) returned none. The Z8 keeps the project's assumed 512 DN
  at 14 bits, labeled `assumed`. The D850's measured 400 DN (research/d850.md) is a different sensor and is not

## Body geometry — Nikon Z-mount

**Flange focal distance / throat diameter / mount hardware** (reported), Wikipedia
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_Z-mount>: flange focal distance **16 mm**, throat (inner)
diameter **55 mm** — "the largest full-frame lens mount," explicitly described there as "much
larger than the older Nikon F-mount (44 mm)." Bayonet mount with **4 tabs** (vs 3 on the F-mount)
and **11 electrical pins** (vs 8 on the F-mount). These figures match exactly what the lead
specified in the task addition (flange 16mm, throat 55mm), now cited to a primary-style reference.

**Viewfinder** (reported): electronic — Quad-VGA OLED EVF, 1280×960, **0.8× magnification** (with a
50mm lens), 3000-nit brightness. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_Z8>. Categorically different
from the D850's optical pentaprism: there is no mirror or prism light path in the Z8 at all, only a
sensor feed to a screen.

**Viewfinder panel size and battery** (spec, added 10/11/2026, score loop r1): Nikon's own Z8 online
manual, Technical Notes > Specifications
(<https://onlinemanual.nikonimglib.com/z8/en/specifications_384.html>, accessed 10/11/2026), lists
the viewfinder as a "1.27-cm/0.5-in. approx. 3690k-dot (Quad VGA) OLED electronic viewfinder", the
camera as "Approx. 144 × 118.5 × 83 mm" (no note on what the depth includes) and the EN-EL15c
battery as "Approx. 40 × 56 × 20.5 mm" (W × H × D). Derived for the model
(`blender/mirrorless_v2.py`): Quad VGA is 1280 × 960, a 4:3 panel, so a 12.7 mm (0.5 in.) diagonal
is 10.16 × 7.62 mm (12.7 × 4/5 and 12.7 × 3/5). The EVF panel was drawn 14 × 10.5 mm (17.5 mm
diagonal, 37% oversize) before this. The D850 takes the EN-EL15a of the same family; the manual
above says the Z8 accepts EN-EL15a and EN-EL15b in place of the EN-EL15c, so both bodies' battery
blocks use the EN-EL15c's 40 × 56 × 20.5 mm.

**Monitor** (spec, added 10/11/2026, score loop r2): the same Nikon page gives the monitor as
"8-cm/3.2-in., approx. 2100k-dot vertically and horizontally tilting TFT touch-sensitive LCD with 170°
viewing angle". It names no pixel format, and no first-party source found this session gives the panel's
aspect ratio (third-party spec sites list only the image aspect ratios), so the model keeps its opening's
aspect (75 × 50.4 mm, about 3:2) and takes only the diagonal from Nikon: 67.5 × 45.3 mm of picture
(`blender/mirrorless_v2.py`). It was 75 × 50.4 mm (3.56 in.) before this. Open: the Z8 panel's aspect.

**Body dimensions / weight** (reported): 144 × 118.5 × 83 mm, 910 g with battery.
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_Z8>. Nikon's own marketing describes the Z8 as "30% smaller
than the Z9" (its full-pro-body sibling); no first-party Nikon spec page was directly fetchable in
this pass (nikonusa.com's `/p/z-8/1695/overview` page did not carry a machine-readable specs table
via this session's fetch tool, and a dedicated `/specifications` sub-page returned HTTP 404;
B&H's product listing page returned HTTP 403). Wikipedia's figures are used as the primary citation,
cross-checked against WebSearch-tool summaries of B&H/DPReview listings that state the same 910 g
figure independently.

**Processor / release / stabilization** (reported): Nikon EXPEED 7 (vs the D850's EXPEED 5),
released 05/26/2023, 5-axis in-body image stabilization (the D850 has none).
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_Z8>

## Contrast with the D850 (explicit, per the lead's request)

| | Nikon D850 | Nikon Z8 |
|---|---|---|
| Sensor architecture | Non-stacked BSI CMOS | Stacked BSI CMOS, parallel readout paths |
| Shutter | Mechanical focal-plane (+ electronic "Silent LV" option) | Electronic only, no mechanical shutter |
| Full-sensor readout | ~64 ms (low confidence, no primary table row found) | 3.7 ms / 1/268 s (secondary: Hogan's measured list, read directly; no Nikon figure or horshack row) |
| Mount | F-mount: 46.5 mm flange, 44 mm throat | Z-mount: 16 mm flange, 55 mm throat |
| Viewfinder | Optical pentaprism, 100% coverage, 0.75x | Electronic Quad-VGA OLED, 0.8x (50mm lens) |
| Full well (photonstophotos, two datasets) | 59,126 / 60,912 e- | 54,323 / 56,277 e- |
| DCG switch | ISO 318 -> 400, -3.76 stops/stop | ISO 400 -> 503, -6.45 stops/stop |
| Base ISO | 64 | 64 |
| Active area / effective MP | 35.9x23.9mm, 45.4MP | 35.9x23.9mm, 45.4MP (same) |
| Body dimensions | 146 x 124 x 78.5 mm | 144 x 118.5 x 83 mm |
| Weight | 915 g body-only / 1005 g w/ battery | 910 g w/ battery (no separate body-only figure found) |
| IBIS | None | 5-axis |
| Processor | EXPEED 5 | EXPEED 7 |
| Flash sync | 1/250 s | 1/200 s base, 1/8000 s high-speed sync |

Both readout-speed figures (64 ms for the D850, ~3.6 ms for the Z8) share the same sourcing
weakness: horshack-dpreview's RollingShutter combined-results table, this project's primary source
for readout timing on every other camera, does not carry a row for either the D850 or the Z8. Both
numbers come from search-tool summaries of blocked or partially-inaccessible secondary pages. The
qualitative contrast (stacked sensor reads out roughly an order of magnitude faster) is corroborated
by several independent sources and by first-principles reasoning about what stacking buys, but
neither exact millisecond figure should be treated as fully verified.

### Grip profile: stylized (score loop r4, 10/11/2026)

The reviewers asked for a sourced finger recess and forward flare on the Z8 grip. A search (10/11/2026; Nikon's
specification page, DPReview, Imaging Resource, retailer listings) found only the overall 144 x 118.5 x 83 mm and no
grip depth, recess or elevation, and Nikon's manual shows the grip front and side only in perspective. The plan is
traced from the top drawing (above); the grip's elevation, its forward bulge and its channel are the model's own
stylized shapes, and none was changed. research/exteriors.md already flags the 38 mm grip depth as `assumed`.

### Grip, hump rake and mount boss: round 6 (score loop r6, 10/11/2026)

Asked once more for a photograph-calibrated grip, hump rake and boss; searched again (10/14/2026, with the D850 search in
research/d850.md): the overall 144 x 118.5 x 83 mm and nothing else, no scaled side or front elevation, no CAD. The Z8's grip,
hump rake and mount boss (71 mm across, about 6 mm tall) remain the model's own stylized shapes, named so in
`public/models/bodies.json` `shapeNote`. What did change is not a dimension: the lock pin and the four screw heads on the mount
ring now sit in 0.5 mm pockets (smaller heads on the Z mount's 2.4 mm wide ring, 0.85 mm in radius against 0.95 and 1.25 mm on the
F mount), so the ring's front is the flange plane (16.0) and it no longer runs 0.75 mm into a mounted lens's rear plate
(1,436 triangle pairs against the z800, m50 and z35).

## First-party specifications (score loop r6, 10/14/2026)

Read directly: the Nikon Z 8 online manual's Specifications page (<https://onlinemanual.nikonimglib.com/z8/en/specifications_375.html>)
and Nikon USA's "Nikon Z Series: Z Mount System" article
(<https://www.nikonusa.com/learn-and-explore/c/products-and-innovation/nikon-z-series-z-mount-system>). Nikon prints: lens mount "Nikon Z
mount"; image sensor "35.9 × 23.9 mm CMOS sensor (full-frame/FX-format)"; effective pixels "45.7 million"; ISO "64–25600 (choose from
step sizes of 1/3 and 1 EV)"; shutter "1/32000 – 30 s (... extendable to 900 s in mode M), bulb, time", electronic shutter with a
sensor shield; flash sync "synchronizes with shutter at speeds of 1/250 or 1/200 s or slower"; dimensions "Approx. 144 × 118.5 ×
83 mm"; weight "Approx. 910 g ... with battery and memory card but without body cap and accessory shoe cover". The Z-mount article:
"The system's large lens mount features a 55mm inner diameter and short 16mm flange focal distance."
`data/z8.json` and `data/hardware/mounts.json` (nikonZ flange and throat) now label those `spec` with these citations, and keep the
earlier Wikipedia source as a `crossCheck`. Not upgraded: pixel pitch (Nikon prints none; derived), the extended ISO 32 to 102400
range (not on the manual page read), and the flash sync value, which keeps 1/200 s (the slower of Nikon's two prints; the page does not
say which settings allow 1/250 s). The readout time is separate: see "Readout / rolling-shutter speed" above (3.7 ms, Hogan's measured
list, secondary).
