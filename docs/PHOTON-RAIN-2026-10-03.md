# Photon rain and shot noise

The Pixel level has a persistent **Photon rain & noise** launcher. The separate experiment retains the workspace lens, aperture, shutter and sensor, then derives local reference illumination for about six expected incident photons per equally lit, on-axis 18% gray pixel. Changing its controls does not change the workspace or an example photograph.

Eight visible pixels receive wavelength-colored individual photons while a 256-pixel patch and count histogram accumulate the same seeded sample. One dot represents one photon. Playback stretches the selected exposure into six seconds; the scale badge gives the physical rate and time factor and identifies the paths as schematic. A 16× light comparison changes the expected mean from 6 to 96 and relative photon shot noise from 40.8% to 10.2%. The patch is normalized to the expected mean, so the reduced relative variation remains visible rather than being hidden by brightness.

Counts use the engine's incident-photon expectation and PCG Poisson sampler. Uniform arrival times conditional on each Poisson count form a homogeneous Poisson process. Wavelength sampling uses daylight spectral power × wavelength × bin width, consistent with photon-number weighting. No photons are inferred from supplied JPEGs. These are incident photons before CFA transmission and quantum efficiency; electron conversion, read noise, dark current, fixed-pattern noise and full-well clipping are explicitly outside this experiment. ISO is not a photon control.

The expected distribution is computed by a stable Poisson recurrence around its mode. Histogram buckets have equal integer count widths, avoiding artificial oscillation from alternating count-bin widths. Scrubbing and replay preserve the seeded sample; **New exposure** advances the seed. Reduced motion opens a complete static exposure, with optional manual playback. Closing, leaving Pixel, hiding the page and disposal stop animation. Async lazy loading checks that Pixel is still active before opening.
